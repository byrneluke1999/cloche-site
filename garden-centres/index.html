What it takes

A label and twenty minutes a month.

From you

  • Tell us which plants you'd like labels for, with the exact plant or cultivar.
  • Put the labels on those pots, at restock or at the till.
  • Say it once at the till: "Scan the label to keep it alive."
  • Twenty minutes a month to tell us how it's going.

From us

  • Make and deliver the labels.
  • Set up each plant's care. Cloche works out the watering, so you never enter or approve it.
  • Keep "Bought at" and your name on every plant, for as long as the customer owns it.
  • A short note each month: plants added, how many people, and how many are still being watered a month later.

Questions

The things shops ask.

Do our customers need to install something?

Yes: Cloche, a free app. It's iPhone first, with Android planned. We'd start once it's on the App Store, and the link on every label tells anyone without the app how to get it.

What does it cost?

Nothing for the pilot. If it works well for you, we'd talk about local offers or a share of sales that start in the app, but only then, and only if you want to.

Who decides how often a plant is watered?

Cloche does, from its own plant-care data, and we check it before any label is printed. You never enter or approve a watering schedule, and customers can still change it for their own home.

What if one of our plants isn't set up?

We add it before printing. A label is only made for a plant whose care is already set up, so every scan fills in.

What do we see about our customers?

Counts only: how many plants were added from your labels, by how many people, and how many are still being watered a month later. Never names, photos or addresses. Our privacy policy has the detail.

What do the labels look like, and which pots do they suit?

A small waterproof sticker, about 5 by 9 cm: the Cloche mark, a QR code, the plant's name and yours. They suit pots from about 12 cm up. For very small pots, tell us and we'll find something that fits.

How many plants should we start with?

A handful: your best sellers and the ones customers ask about. Five to fifteen is plenty for a first run.

What if it isn't for us?

Stop whenever you like. You'd have lost a few stickers and a little time.

Who is behind Cloche?

Luke Byrne, a Dublin developer. Cloche is a small independent project, and you'd deal with me directly.
